# Retrospective

## Objective

Capture what happened during a delivery or sprint cycle, what the team learned, and what should change for the next iteration.

## When to use it

Use this template after a sprint, project milestone, release window, or major delivery period when reflection and improvement are appropriate.

## Responsible

- Engineering Manager or delivery lead: facilitate the retrospective
- Product Manager: contribute business and priority feedback
- Team members: provide observed lessons and follow-ups

## Required fields

- Period or cycle name
- Successes
- Frictions or pain points
- Root causes
- Actions to improve
- Owner for each action
- Follow-up date

## Optional fields

- What should remain unchanged
- Risks or unaddressed dependencies
- Communication issues
- Quality or delivery learnings

## Complete example

```md
# Retrospective: Sprint 9

## Objective

Identify delivery patterns and quality gaps that need to improve in the next cycle.

## When to use it

Use after a sprint closes or after a release that required coordination between product, engineering, and operations.

## Responsible

- Engineering Manager: facilitate the discussion.
- Product Manager: provide product outcome context.
- Team members: share observed friction and action suggestions.

## Required fields

- Period or cycle name: Sprint 9
- Successes: test evidence was well attached to the release record
- Frictions or pain points: security review timing created a late dependency
- Root causes: design handoff was too late for the sprint window
- Actions to improve: move architecture review earlier and create a formal security entry gate
- Owner for each action: CTO and QA lead
- Follow-up date: next sprint planning
```

## Checklist

- [ ] the retrospective covers roadblocks, not just wins
- [ ] actions are specific and owned
- [ ] follow-up timing is visible
- [ ] learning is linked to delivery or quality behavior

## Common errors

- recording only positive feedback and skipping the real blockers
- turning actions into vague intentions
- leaving follow-up ownership unresolved

## Best practices

- keep feedback evidence-backed and specific
- separate event symptoms from systemic causes
- convert discussions into concrete next-step ownership
